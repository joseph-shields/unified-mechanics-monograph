# Unified Mechanics

**Unified Mechanics** is a theoretical framework built around a simple question:

> **What information must a physical system preserve in order to determine its admissible future?**

The framework begins from a future-determination tautology: two physical descriptions are equivalent when no admissible future continuation or readout can distinguish them. Requiring the **coarsest lossless representation** turns that statement into a mathematical constraint on physical state.

From this foundation, Unified Mechanics develops a connected structure for state, dynamics, self-description, boundary, action, observation, persistence, and physical realisation.

## Core Idea

Let a physical carrier have a declared set of admissible continuations and readouts.

Two carriers are identified when every declared future produces the same law:

\[
c \sim_{\mathcal T} c'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal L_\tau(c)=\mathcal L_\tau(c')
\qquad
\forall \tau\in\mathcal T.
\]

The resulting quotient

\[
\mathcal Q_{\mathcal T}
=
\mathcal C / {\sim_{\mathcal T}}
\]

is the coarsest state space that preserves every distinction capable of affecting the declared future.

The central dependency is

\[
\text{future determination}
\rightarrow
\text{physical state}
\rightarrow
\text{induced dynamics}
\rightarrow
\text{persistent realisation}
\rightarrow
\text{least necessary boundary}.
\]

The theory therefore treats state and dynamics as parts of the same construction rather than independent ingredients.

## Reflexive Closure

A distinguished sector appears when a system must retain both its present resolution and its immediately retained context.

Under minimal two-channel, memory-one, exact-copy conditions, the update becomes

\[
(R,C)\mapsto(R+C,R),
\]

with matrix

\[
F=
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1\\
1 & 0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its positive projective fixed ratio is the golden ratio,

\[
\varphi^2=\varphi+1.
\]

For the two-sided physical realisation used in Unified Mechanics,

\[
r=\frac{1}{2\varphi},
\]

giving the exact decomposition

\[
1=(1-r)^2+2r(1-r)+r^2.
\]

These three channels are interpreted within the physical framework as

\[
W_L=(1-r)^2,
\qquad
W_B=2r(1-r),
\qquad
W_M=r^2,
\]

corresponding respectively to **Light**, **Boundary**, and **Matter** sectors.

The golden ratio is therefore not introduced as an arbitrary numerical fit. It appears within a specifically defined reflexive closure problem.

## Least-Boundary Principle

Once the future-sufficient state is physically realised as a finite persistent record, Unified Mechanics proposes an additional physical principle:

> **Among admissible histories preserving the same future-active distinctions, physical realisation minimises cumulative dynamically necessary boundary.**

This connects the abstract state construction to geometric and field-theoretic descriptions.

The resulting programme investigates whether familiar physical structures can be understood as different realisations of the same underlying state–continuation–boundary architecture.

## Scientific Status

Unified Mechanics deliberately separates different levels of claim:

- **Exact theorems** — mathematical consequences of explicitly stated premises.
- **Derived consequences** — results following from earlier established structure.
- **Physical principles** — proposed laws of physical realisation.
- **Realisation bridges** — identifications connecting mathematical structure to physical quantities or carriers.
- **Empirical results** — completed numerical or observational comparisons.
- **Open tests** — predictions or calculations that remain independently testable.

This distinction is important because the logical foundation of the framework can be exact without every proposed physical identification being established automatically.

The purpose of the project is not to obtain agreement by fitting every sector independently. It is to determine how far a common structural law can be carried before observation forces it to fail or be revised.
