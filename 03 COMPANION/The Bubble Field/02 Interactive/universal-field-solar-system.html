Method, meaning and limits

This map combines approximate Keplerian planetary positions with an exploratory rendering of Joseph Shields’ formalised field programme. It deliberately separates established astronomy from framework-specific diagnostics.

Established astronomical inputs

  • Planetary positions use the JPL approximate Keplerian elements valid for 1800–2050.
  • Planet masses, radii and mean orbital distances use NASA comparative data.
  • Orbits are projected for readability; object sizes and closure bubbles are not distance-scale physical spheres.

Framework mapping

  • φ: a rendered scalar proxy, available only in the potentiated case.
  • ψᵢ bubbles: local closures, shown recursively as planet and schematic sub-closure layers.
  • 𝒟(x,y): an antisymmetric comparison. With zero curvature it equals φ(x)−φ(y).
  • Loop residue: a toy line integral of a rotational scattering term. Non-zero residue demonstrates what a scalar map cannot contain; it is not an observed anomaly.

What “normal physics does not recover” means here

The site does not claim that established physics is unable to model radiation, gravity, hierarchy or scattering. The narrower claim is that a single scalar potential cannot encode path-dependent distinctions. Full radiative transfer and vector/connection descriptions can. The Universal Field of Least Action reframes those quantities as closure depth, relational boundaries, holonomy efficiency and exactness obstruction.
