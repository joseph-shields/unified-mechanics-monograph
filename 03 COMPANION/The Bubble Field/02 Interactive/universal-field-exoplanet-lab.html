Directly recovered from orbital geometry

μ = GM is obtained from period and semi-major axis:

μᵢ = 4π²aᵢ³ / Pᵢ²

The ensemble then tests whether one source-scaled law compresses many systems, and whether the freely fitted exponents approach mass¹ and radius⁻².

Recovered by the visual field frame

For every selected system the map reconstructs radial and tangential motion, angular momentum, areal rate, energy, torque, curvature and the angle between v and g. Standard mechanics contains these; the field interface makes them simultaneous projections of one local relation.

Why the catalogue does not independently measure G

Astronomical orbits determine GM. Catalogue stellar masses in solar units are themselves model-dependent, and many semi-major axes are calculated using Kepler’s law. Therefore the displayed G is conditional on the absolute solar-mass value entered above—not an independent laboratory determination.

The framework would break the degeneracy only by deriving an absolute mass in kilograms without using gravity. Then G = μ/M would be a genuine consequence.
